Experimental `sophia_shell_v1` revision 1 now fixes the title-only descriptor
switcher lifecycle. The broker emits stable, nonreused action tokens bound to
the descriptor generation. The shell wire narrows them again to exact broker,
revocation, recipient, slot, and target generations. Complete snapshots disclose
opaque slots, sanitized labels, trust, and attention; they contain no surface
identity, coordinates, icons, PID, path, class, namespace identity, or raw
input.

The shell returns one bounded ordering, selection, and visibility candidate.
Engine privately resolves it, performs layout and rendering, publishes the hit
targets, captures the pointer, and creates the exact presented activation.
Prepared and presented outcomes remain distinct. Queue saturation and ordinary
disconnect revoke the recipient interaction epoch immediately; the model permits
the prior pixels to remain while forbidding capture or activation through them.
TLC exhaustively checked 23,582,243 generated and 241,549 distinct states to
depth 20 without an invariant violation.

The standalone Nim `hagia-shell` shares no policy process or codec. Its reducer
owns deterministic order and selection, consumes an activation at most once,
and handles complete withdrawal. Rust and Nim accept the seven-frame golden
corpus and reject all twelve malformed frames. Independent C and Hagia clients
both complete the same Bubblewrap-protected cross-process proof through
presentation, Engine hit testing, activation acknowledgement, and withdrawal.
The proof reports zero disclosed surface ids, coordinates, and icons.

This is an offline interface experiment, not an installed feature or a stable
revision. It adds no live shortcut, shell launch, issuer-side action dispatch,
reconnect loop, preview, icon transfer, work-area reservation, or compatibility
promise. The next critical-path slice is live shortcut admission and action
dispatch through this exact boundary; reservation/WM coordination follows only
after the no-reservation lifecycle is live.
